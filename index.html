Abstract

While traditional modeling effectively supports a robot's intrinsic mass, providing active anti-gravity assistance for the combined mass of a coupled human-exoskeleton system remains notoriously difficult. Challenges such as non-rigid interfacing, unmodeled biological torques, and the static indeterminacy of closed kinematic loops severely limit the efficacy of analytical modeling. In this paper, we present a novel, efficient data collection paradigm that bypasses these complexities to directly learn effective feedforward assistance torques. By implementing a velocity-limited admittance controller, we establish a kinematic constraint that actively suppresses inertial dynamics, Coriolis effects, and voluntary human reflexes. This generates a clean dataset isolating configuration-dependent assistive requirements. We use this data to produce an empirical model that successfully generalizes gravity-compensation scaling to novel subjects. Our model drives absolute torque prediction errors below 10% and significantly outperforms state-of-the-art analytical baselines derived from sparse wearable sensors. Finally, our few-shot calibration protocol extracts user-specific assistive profiles, allowing the personalized model to scale to high-acceleration dynamic tasks and providing a robust foundation for high-force assistance.
